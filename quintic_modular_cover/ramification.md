# The quintic modular cover and its two-subset resolvent

Let H and G be generated by the literal row-major matrices in
`group_theory_40.m`, at common level 40. They represent
`40.480.33.ck.1` and `40.96.1.o.2`, respectively. The script checks inclusion
with the identity conjugator, full determinant image, and membership of
`-I` in both groups. Their orders are 1536 and 7680, their determinant-one
orders are 96 and 480, and their geometric projective orders are 48 and 240.
Thus the coarse map has geometric degree 5. Full determinant gives
geometrically connected modular curves over Q, and the inclusion gives the
map over Q. Passing to smooth compactifications extends this map uniquely.

Reduction modulo 5 maps G onto GL2(F5). The image H5 has order 96, and the
script checks the equality

```text
H = { g in G : g mod 5 belongs to H5 }.
```

It also checks conjugacy of H5 with the actual subgroup in `5.5.0.a.1`.
Consequently X_H is the normalization of the fiber product of X_G with
that degree-five cover of the j-line, using the recorded conjugation to
identify representatives. The induced coset action is transitive after
restriction to G intersect SL2, so this fiber product has one geometric
component. Its function-field degree over Q(X_G) is five; no smaller
component has been selected from a reducible plane model.

The five-letter coset representation has arithmetic image S5 and geometric
image A5. Its geometric cycle types and element counts are

| Cycle type | Number |
| --- | ---: |
| 1^5 | 1 |
| 3 1^2 | 20 |
| 2^2 1 | 15 |
| 5 | 24 |

In particular this degree-five cover is not geometrically Galois. Its
Galois closure has a nontrivial constant-field extension: the discriminant
calculation below identifies the quadratic sign field as Q(sqrt(5)).
The arithmetic group must not be substituted for the geometric group.

The target has index 96, no elliptic points of orders 2 or 3, and cusp
widths consisting of eight 4s and eight 8s. Its genus is
`1 + 96/12 - 16/2 = 1`. The source has index 480, no elliptic points, and
eight cusp widths 20 and eight 40; its genus is
`1 + 480/12 - 16/2 = 33`. All these values are computed from the standard
S, R and T coset permutations, rather than read from the labels.

The primary j-map and its exact identities are

```text
j5(t)        = 4(4t-1)^3(4t^2+3t+9),
j5(t)-1728   = 4(t-1)(16t^2+8t+21)^2,
j5'(t)       = 20(4t-1)^2(16t^2+8t+21),
disc_t(j5-j) = 2^40 * 5^5 * j^2 * (j-1728)^2.
```

Factorization gives geometric partitions `(3,1,1)`, `(2,2,1)`, and `(5)`
over 0, 1728, and infinity. The discriminant has squareclass 5 over Q(j)
and becomes a square over algebraically closed constants, consistent with
the two computed monodromy groups. It is a polynomial-discriminant identity;
the trace-discriminant divisor of the normalized cover is computed next.

In characteristic zero, a local branch of index r under normalized base
change of index e splits into gcd(r,e) branches of index r/gcd(r,e).
This follows by normalizing `u^r=v^e` over algebraically closed residue
fields. The target j-map has index 3 everywhere above 0 and index 2
everywhere above 1728, because its S and R coset permutations have no fixed
points. These finite ramifications therefore disappear after normalization.
At each target cusp, e is 4 or 8, coprime to 5. There is exactly one point
above it, with ramification index 5. Hence, writing Br for the reduced
branch divisor,

```text
D_psi = 4 Br(psi),   B_psi = empty,
deg D_psi = 16*4 = 64,
2g(X_H)-2 = 64,      g(X_H) = 33.
```

This independent Riemann–Hurwitz calculation agrees with the source's
modular-coset genus. The script implements the normalized branch-index
calculation and recomputes the target indices directly from its generators.

For the resolvent, let the arithmetic S5 act on the ten unordered
two-element subsets of its five letters. The corresponding stabilizer
defines a degree-ten cover of X_G over Q. Its geometric subgroup A5 acts
transitively, so this resolvent is geometrically connected, even though
the Galois closure has nontrivial constant field. A 5-cycle acts as two
5-cycles on the subsets, with permutation index 8. Thus this cover is
branched only at the same sixteen cusps and

```text
2g(R)-2 = 16*8 = 128,    g(R) = 65.
```

The resolvent is defined intrinsically by this permutation action. No
identification with a named modular curve is needed or asserted.

The rational cusp and Jacobian/newform inputs are separately documented in
[LMFDB_inputs.md](LMFDB_inputs.md). They identify the pointed target's
Jacobian up to Q-isogeny with class 800.d. The explicit non-torsion point
in `ramification.m` gives positive algebraic rank in that class; the
separate [elliptic rank script](../elliptic_targets.m) computes an algebraic
upper bound, as explained in [the rank note](../elliptic_targets.md). Rank
transfers to the pointed target by Q-isogeny.

For completeness, the application uses the following deductions, rather
than a database output. Faltings gives finitely many rational points on
X_H and R, since both genera exceed one. Away from the finitely many
branch fibers, a reducible quintic fiber either has a linear factor or
has factorization type 2+3. The first case lifts to X_H(Q), and the second
to R(Q). Therefore all but finitely many rational fibers are irreducible.
The target's positive rank gives infinitely many such fibers. If only
finitely many field isomorphism classes occurred, their Galois closures
would have one finite compositum over which X_H has infinitely many
points, again contradicting Faltings.

Every quintic field is primitive, and `33 > 5^2+1`. The existing main
theorems therefore apply with empty odd-discriminant support. Their
square-root bound counts distinct discriminants, not fields. Generic S5
monodromy does not assert that every irreducible specialization has group
S5. These observations make no stronger quantitative assertion.
